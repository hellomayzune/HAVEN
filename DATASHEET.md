# 🏠 Dataset A — Room-level monitoring record (`NottinghamMeasuredTRH.xlsx`)

## Motivation

**For what purpose was the dataset created?**

The dataset was created to provide a continuous hourly record of temperature and humidity across the rooms of an occupied dwelling over multiple warm seasons. Within HAVEN, it is used to quantify room-level night-time heat exposure, identify indoor heat episodes, characterise post-episode thermal recovery, assess outdoor heat conditions at the dwelling, and develop and evaluate room-level forecasting models for hot-night warnings.

**Who created the dataset?**

The monitoring record was collected in a single case-study dwelling in the East Midlands, UK, which is the private residence of one of the authors. Data collection was undertaken by that author, while the subsequent analysis was conducted by the authors. The dataset was not created on behalf of any commercial or institutional third party.

**Who funded its creation?**

No specific grant was received from any funding agency.

## Composition

**What do the instances represent?**

Each instance represents an hourly observation of dry-bulb air temperature (°C), relative humidity (%) and dew-point temperature (°C) at one of eight monitored locations within a four-bedroom detached dwelling. These comprise one outdoor location and seven indoor zones. The workbook has one sheet per location: `External`, `Kitchen_NW`, `Living_SE`, `Hall`, `Landing`, `MasterBed_SE`, `Bed4_SE` and `Bed3_NW`. The suffix gives the principal orientation of the room; the circulation spaces (`Hall`, `Landing`) have none. The bedrooms (`MasterBed_SE`, `Bed4_SE`, `Bed3_NW`) are the sleeping zones.

**How many instances are there?**

Each sheet covers the period from 1 May 2023 00:00 to 2 October 2026 00:00 on an hourly grid (30,001 hourly timestamps). The loader detects the header row and the time, temperature, humidity and dew-point columns on each sheet.

**Is it a sample?**

It is the complete logged record, without downsampling. The `Hall` and `Bed4_SE` loggers were installed later, so their records begin on 2 July 2023 (1,488 fewer measured hours).

**What does each instance consist of?**

The values exported from the loggers comprise the timestamp, temperature, relative humidity and dew-point temperature. Every derived quantity — psychrometric variables, features, exposure and recovery indicators, episodes and warnings — is computed by the notebooks.

**Is there a label or target?**

No explicit labels are supplied in the original dataset. In Notebook 04, the forecasting target is the air temperature in each indoor zone at lead times of 1–48 h. In Notebook 06, a hot-night event is defined as a complete measured bedroom night containing at least one hourly temperature observation above 26 °C; warnings are evaluated against this event definition.

**Is any information missing?**

The pre-installation period of the `Hall` and `Bed4_SE` loggers and a few isolated one- to two-hour gaps. Missing and imputed values are listed in `imputation_log.csv` and marked in the provenance mask.

**Are relationships between instances explicit?**

All sheets share the time index. Notebook 01 aligns them into one hourly frame with one column per location and variable.

**Are there recommended data splits?**

Chronological splits only, with no target crossing a boundary. The reported run trains to 30 April 2025, validates on 1 May–30 November 2025 and tests from 1 December 2025; a second fold trains to 30 April 2024, validates on May–November 2024 and tests on December 2024–November 2025.

**Errors, noise or redundancy?**

Measurement uncertainty is characterised by the manufacturers' stated logger specifications. Notebook 01 identifies observations outside predefined physical bounds and flags implausible hour-to-hour changes. The outdoor logger records higher temperatures than the nearest Met Office synoptic station; this difference is quantified in Notebook 02. 

**Self-contained?**

Yes. The analyses refer to external standards (CIBSE TM59, the Met Office heatwave definition, the Environment Canada Humidex classes), which are cited rather than redistributed.

**Confidential data?**

The record contains no personal, health or financial information, but it comes from an occupied private home and is treated as confidential (`ADR-008`).

## Collection process

**How was the data acquired?**

Data were collected through continuous automated logging. Indoor environmental conditions were measured using Tinytag Ultra 2 loggers (TGU-4500), installed at heights as close as practicable to 1.10 m above floor level. Actual installation heights ranged from 0.8 to 1.3 m. Outdoor conditions were recorded with an IP68-rated Tinytag Plus 2 logger (TGP-4500). Both loggers have a temperature resolution of 0.01 °C and, from the manufacturer's accuracy curve, an accuracy of about ±0.4–0.5 °C between 0 and 40 °C (about ±0.6 °C at −10 °C); relative-humidity resolution is better than 0.3% RH, with an accuracy of ±3.0% RH at 25 °C. The siting, mounting height and radiation shielding of the outdoor logger are not reported.

**Mechanism.**

Automatic logging at fixed one-hour intervals, with no manual transcription.

**Timeframe.**

1 May 2023 to 1 October 2026, covering four complete May–September seasons (2023–2026); the Hall and Bedroom 4 records begin on 2 July 2023.

**Who was involved?**

The household maintained its usual domestic routines and received no instructions regarding window operation, shading, fan use or heating. Occupancy patterns and the operation of windows, shading devices and fans were not monitored or recorded. The dwelling has no fixed mechanical cooling. A portable air conditioner was used in the kitchen area during the daytime in the 2026 heatwaves; its operation was not logged.

## Preprocessing / cleaning / labelling

Notebook 01 constructs a regular hourly data frame and applies quality-control procedures to identify observations outside predefined physical bounds (−15 to 45 °C outdoors, −5 to 45 °C indoors, and 0–100% RH) and observations exhibiting hour-to-hour changes exceeding separate thresholds for the indoor and outdoor loggers. Dew-point temperature is recalculated following any applicable corrections. Missing observations are subsequently addressed using PCHIP interpolation for interior gaps of up to 24 h and donor-zone regression for longer gaps and the 2023 boundary gaps in the `Hall` (donor `Living_SE`) and `Bed4_SE` (donor `MasterBed_SE`) zones. A provenance mask (`01_qc_flags.parquet`) records corrected and imputed values. Exposure, episode, recovery, TM59 and warning-outcome indicators use measured values only; the complete record is used for forecasting inputs. The raw workbook is never modified.

## Uses

**Used for:**

Night-time exposure (I_night, exceedance and no-relief nights) by room and year; CIBSE TM59 Criterion (b) applied to measured air temperature; indoor heat episodes, event envelopes and timing relative to heatwaves; recovery endpoints and decay half-lives with their reliability; the on-site heatwave catalogue and the site–station comparison; multi-horizon room-level forecasts and hot-night warnings with their evaluation.

**Other possible uses:**

Testing other night-time or adaptive comfort indicators; benchmarking indoor temperature forecasting methods; studying differences between rooms in one dwelling.

**Limits on future use.**

As the monitoring record was obtained from a single dwelling, the findings constitute within-dwelling empirical evidence and cannot be assumed to generalise to buildings with different construction characteristics, orientations, occupancy patterns or climatic conditions. Only air temperature and humidity were measured: mean radiant temperature, window state, ventilation rates and internal gains were not.

**Should not be used:**

As a representative sample of the UK housing stock; as a formal compliance assessment under CIBSE TM59 or Approved Document O; or in any attempt to identify the dwelling or its occupants.

## Distribution and maintenance

**Distribution.**

**Not publicly distributed**, to honour confidentiality agreements and protect the occupants' privacy (`ADR-008`). The structure documented above allows the notebooks to be run on another dataset of the same form.

**Maintenance.**

Maintained by the study authors. New months are added by re-exporting the workbook; `config.find_raw_workbook()` uses `NottinghamMeasuredTRH.xlsx` when present and otherwise the most recently modified workbook in `data/raw/`.

# 📡 Dataset B — Met Office MIDAS Open hourly observations (CSV)

`midas-open_uk-hourly-weather-obs_dv-202607_nottinghamshire_00554_sutton-bonington_qcv-1_<year>.csv` for 2023, 2024 and 2025

## Motivation

Dataset B provides an independent reference for outdoor meteorological conditions from the nearest Met Office synoptic station. It enables heatwaves to be identified independently using an official meteorological record and facilitates comparison with the on-site outdoor measurements.

## Composition

Hourly observations at station 00554 (Sutton Bonington) in BADC-CSV format: a metadata header ending in a `data` line, hourly rows and an `end data` line. The variables read are the observation time (`ob_time`, UTC), air temperature (`air_temperature`) and its quality flag (`air_temperature_q`), relative humidity (`rltv_hum`), dew point (`dewpoint`), wind speed (`wind_speed`, knots) and hourly sunshine duration (`wmo_hr_sun_dur`, or `drv_hr_sun_dur` where the former is missing). Dataset version `dv-202607`, quality-control version 1. Files for 2026 were not available at the time of analysis.

## Preprocessing

Notebook 02 reads every file matching `midas-open_uk-hourly-weather-obs*.csv` in `data/raw/midas/`, removes duplicate times, places the observations on an hourly grid and moves them +1 h onto the logger clock (`ADR-005`). Hours with quality code 106 are listed and retained (`ADR-014`). A station day enters the heatwave rule only with at least 20 hourly values.

## Uses and limits

**Used for:**

The station heatwave catalogue; the share of bedroom exposure on station heatwave nights; the site-minus-station temperature difference by wind and sunshine; the time-alignment check.

**Limits.**

Dataset B comprises observations from a single open-site agricultural meteorological station with a grass surface, located some distance from the monitored dwelling. As the comparison is based on only two measurement locations, any neighbourhood-scale urban heat-island effect cannot be distinguished from differences attributable to sensor siting or radiation shielding. The comparison is restricted to the period 2023–2025.

## Distribution and maintenance

Third-party data, **not redistributed**. MIDAS Open is published by the Centre for Environmental Data Analysis ([archive.ceda.ac.uk](https://archive.ceda.ac.uk/)) under the Open Government Licence, which requires attribution citing the dataset version. Download the annual files and place them unmodified in `data/raw/midas/`.

## Constraints carried into every derived table

The night-time exposure index (I_night), exceedance and no-relief nights, indoor heat episodes and the 26 °C threshold are study-specific analytical constructs. TM59 results calculated using measured air temperature are indicative only and do not constitute formal compliance assessments under CIBSE TM59 or Approved Document O.

Thermal recovery indicators describe the observed response of an occupied, predominantly free-running room to the weather conditions following each indoor heat episode. The estimated decay half-life should not be interpreted as a building thermal time constant; its associated reliability is reported alongside the estimate.

Hot-night warnings and associated probabilities are evaluated retrospectively using held-out test periods. No warnings were issued to the household. Passive measures presented in the worked example are illustrative suggestions and have not been evaluated as interventions.

# 📌 Cross-cutting notes

**Single-dwelling scope.**

All data describe one four-bedroom detached house of masonry construction in the East Midlands, UK, built to the 2021 Part L standard, with a design air permeability of 6.2 m³/h·m² at 50 Pa, roof eaves of about 45 cm, top-hung windows opening to about 45°, and acoustic glazing in the bedrooms. Envelope U-values: external walls 0.25 W/m²K, ceiling to loft 0.12 W/m²K, suspended ground floor 0.12 W/m²K, windows 1.57 W/m²K.

**Personal data.**

The repository contains no information that identifies the occupants. Sensor locations are given only as room type, floor and principal orientation.

**Licensing.**

Code under the MIT Licence; the tables, figures and model files in `outputs/` and `models/` under CC BY 4.0. Dataset A and the files in `data/processed/` are not distributed. Dataset B is third-party data under the Open Government Licence and is not redistributed.

**Versioning.**

Updates are made by adding new data files and through repository version control, not by editing input files in place. This datasheet is updated with each release.
